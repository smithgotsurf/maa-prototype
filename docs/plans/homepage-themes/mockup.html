Visitor theme switch

Scroll to the bottom of the preview. The footer has a Theme switch with the three curved heroes. It shows on A, B and C; Today is the live site, which doesn't have one.

  • Visitors pick the homepage hero: Home Plate (A), Four Fields (B) or Gold Lines (C). All three use paper grain in the margins. The choice is saved in their browser, so it sticks between visits on that device. First-time visitors see whichever theme you set as the default.
  • Subpages get a short header band with the page title instead of a hero. The band's art is set per page, not by the visitor's theme: Field Rentals uses the ballfield photo, and every other subpage uses Gold Line Art. Four Fields is too busy at that height. Use the Page buttons to see Field Rentals and FAQ.
  • Since subpages don't follow the theme, the switch only shows in the homepage footer.
  • On phones the band is about a third shorter, with a smaller title.
  • Each theme is one entry in a short list: a name, a swatch and a hero. A sport-specific theme later is one more entry plus its image.
  • The theme list and hero live in src/pages/home/ and the switch in src/components/Footer.tsx, both shared with maa-prototype. The band would be a small shared header component; each subpage passes it its title and which art to use.

A single photo that shows every sport

If you'd rather keep a real photo in the hero, here are three ways to get one. Any of them drops straight into the Curved Photo hero.

Shoot it at the Meadow fields

Line up a baseball, softball, basketball, soccer ball and volleyball on home plate or along the first-base chalk line. Shoot low from the dirt in late-afternoon light, wide and in landscape.

Free, local, and no other league has it. A phone camera is enough at this darkness level.

Use your own season photos

One action shot per sport from coaches or parents, placed in the four Four Fields panels instead of the drawings.

Use shots where kids can't be identified, or get a parent's OK for each photo.

Free stock photos

Unsplash and Pexels allow free use on a site like this. Try searches like “sports balls on grass”, “sports equipment flat lay” or “assorted sports balls”.

Pick a landscape photo at least 2000px wide.

What makes a photo work in this hero: the name sits top-center and the Welcome card covers the bottom-center. Look for a photo whose subject sits toward the sides, or that reads as an even texture across the frame. Busy detail right behind the name fights the gold letters.

Building it in both repos

Whichever you pick goes into maa-frontend first, then maa-prototype.

  • src/pages/home/Hero.tsx and public/ are on the shared list, so they copy across unchanged.
  • src/index.css isn't byte-identical (the prototype adds table styles), so a background change there is the same edit made in both files.
  • The margin textures (grain, stripes, ball pattern) are a texture on body plus a plain bg-base-200 and thin gold edge on each page's max-w-4xl wrapper. Those page folders are shared; home/index.tsx is not, so it gets edited in both repos.
  • Margin textures only show on screens wider than about 900px. On phones the content fills the width, so the background is plain tan there.
  • At the top, the content column's background tucks up under the curved hero so its edges meet the curve cleanly.
  • The content column should run all the way down to the footer. Otherwise the gap above the footer shows a band of texture across the full width, which looks like a mistake on phones.
